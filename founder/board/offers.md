# Offers lens — GroupTable

**Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF**

This vote supports a small validation effort. The organiser, employer and caterer are possible payers, but none is validated, and software pricing is unknown. The score reflects missing evidence, not proven absence of demand. This independently applied published framework does not represent its author's opinion.

## What is strong

The implemented workflow supports a concrete desired result: an organiser can obtain an editable meal allocation within confirmed dietary counts, stock and a delivery-inclusive budget. Independent server validation and an immutable approved snapshot make the promised result more credible than an unchecked recommendation alone. Preserved manual choices through simulated checkout and reload help ensure the order matches organiser approval.

The potential value is reduced coordination effort and greater confidence in an order. Those benefits are plausible inferences, not measured outcomes. Reported technical checks establish neither customer confidence nor time savings or willingness to pay. The explicit treatment of missing replies and unknown dietary attributes is sensible, although an offer must never imply verified allergen or cross-contact safety.

## What would kill it — ranked risks

1. **No sufficiently valuable buying occasion — unknown.** We do not know which organiser repeats this task, how costly mistakes or delays are, who controls a budget, or whether that person is reachable. Occasional inconvenience may support a free convenience tool without supporting a paid offer. Demo transaction amounts cannot establish software pricing.

2. **User effort consumes the benefit — inference to test.** Someone must collect and reconcile dietary replies, confirm exclusive segments, and review the result. Optional restaurant searches and branch selection add work before allocation. Four real Qloo requests showed that cuisine associations can change choices; no diner or organiser evidence shows that this improves the desired outcome over choosing a cuisine directly. A technically richer process may leave the buyer's total workload unchanged.

3. **The promised outcome extends beyond controlled capabilities — unknown.** A planner can approve a valid demo allocation, but a delivered team meal depends on a real supplier, current information and fulfilment. If customers value a completed ordering service while GroupTable sells planning software, confidence in the offer will be limited. This requires clarifying the promise before building fulfilment infrastructure.

## Questions for the founder

- Which recurring meal occasion and specific budget holder should the first paid offer address?
- What does that buyer do today, and which observed delay, error or coordination burden makes switching worthwhile?
- What outcome can the team reliably promise within its current scope, and what purchasing commitment would count as validation?

## Conditions for further investment

Recruit five real organisers from one narrowly defined recurring use case. Observe a recent ordering workflow and record its frequency, active work time, errors and purchasing authority. Continue only if at least three describe the same material recurring burden and agree to test GroupTable on their next comparable event. This exploratory gate cannot establish market size.

Run three uncoached, comparable planning trials against each organiser's existing method. Measure total active time, including input preparation and corrections, and record unacceptable allocations. Before testing, set a useful improvement threshold with participants. Compare direct cuisine selection with restaurant-derived taste separately. Retain the extra taste steps only if participants prefer the resulting choices enough to justify the effort.

Then present one explicitly scoped paid pilot offer with a disclosed test price and payer. Seek two genuine purchasing commitments, subject to whatever procurement approval applies. The test price remains undecided.

## Hackathon implication

The prototype can demonstrate a credible constrained allocation and organiser approval flow while showing how Qloo-derived associations influence choices. Keep that claim precise and show the extra taste steps' purpose. Unverified real-model extraction, Sandbox payment and unfamiliar-user completion remain demonstration gaps. Commercial uncertainty does not prevent a useful hackathon prototype, and successful demonstration would not establish an investable offer or competition eligibility.
