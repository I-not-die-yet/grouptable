# GroupTable — Monopoly lens

Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

This subjective commercial assessment applies the published Monopoly framework; it is not its authors’ verdict. Fund a bounded search for a defensible initial niche. The implemented coordination workflow earns that investigation; neither customer demand nor a distinctive commercial advantage is established.

## What is strong

GroupTable addresses a specific coordination task: one organiser must reconcile dietary counts, stock, budget and meal quantities. Confirming mutually exclusive segments, preserving manual edits and independently validating the approved order form a coherent workflow. That is a plausible starting point for differentiation if frequent organisers currently spend substantial effort performing these checks themselves. This remains a hypothesis about customers, not measured time savings.

The optional taste path also has observable behaviour. The brief reports that two Qloo-derived profiles changed meal selections while event, budget and menu stayed constant. It demonstrates an influence on allocation, with dietary requirements remaining hard constraints. It does not establish better recommendations. Keeping the core flow usable when taste lookup fails preserves a useful validation boundary.

Four first-year students have assembled a local prototype with reported technical checks, supporting cheap validation. Buyer access, catering expertise and distribution advantage remain unproven.

## Three ranked risks

1. **The initial niche is too broad — unknown.** “An organiser coordinating colleagues' meals” does not identify ordering frequency, decision authority or a repeatable route to customers. Occasional team lunches may support a different business from recurring operational meal orders. If no concentrated group experiences frequent, costly coordination failures, acquiring each customer could consume more effort than the workflow saves. No software price or willingness to pay is known.

2. **Differentiation may be easy to substitute — inference.** The value could reside mainly in constraint checking and an orderly approval record. Chat plus spreadsheets, direct cuisine selection and supplier-assisted ordering are alternative workflows to test; their actual performance is undocumented. Qloo access alone does not demonstrate proprietary advantage, and the five house-authored dish bridges are coarse. If organisers obtain equivalent acceptable orders with fewer steps elsewhere, the restaurant-profile sequence adds little reason to switch.

3. **A repeat-use advantage has no demonstrated accumulation path — unknown.** The demo has one fictional supplier and no proven customer channel. Repeated use might eventually improve trusted catalogue mappings, organiser routines or supplier relationships, but these are possible directions, not existing assets. Real catalogue upkeep and fulfilment could become necessary before the benefit is repeatable. A mature moat is unnecessary now; a credible learning advantage from repeated orders would justify further investment. No evidence establishes a special timing window.

## Decision-changing founder questions

- Which reachable organiser segment orders repeatedly, and who can approve payment for solving its coordination problem?
- What recurring failure would make that segment replace its existing workflow?
- What becomes materially more useful after its fifth order, and why would the customer keep that value here?

## Conditions for further investment

Recruit five real organisers from one candidate segment. Reconstruct their last orders before demonstrating GroupTable, recording frequency, steps, mistakes and purchasing authority. Observe three uncoached comparisons using realistic order scenarios against each organiser's usual workflow. Continue if at least two complete a valid approved order with less organiser effort and request use on a subsequent real planning occasion. These are proposed gates, not demand estimates.

Separately compare direct cuisine choice with restaurant-based taste input. Retain the latter as commercial differentiation only if organisers prefer its resulting orders enough to justify the extra work. Seek one credible buyer commitment to a defined pilot; price remains an input to test. Defer broad integrations and merchant operations until these signals exist.

## Hackathon implication

Demonstrate the constraint-preserving workflow and honestly labelled Qloo influence. Explain one organiser niche and the next human test. The prototype can support that demonstration without a mature moat or live fulfilment. Competition eligibility, agent compliance, payment readiness and winning prospects require separate evidence.
