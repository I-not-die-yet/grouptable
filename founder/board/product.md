# GroupTable — Product lens

**Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF**

This independently applies the published Product lens without attributing a verdict to its source author. The commercial proposition merits further effort if organisers reach useful results with less work than their current method. Customer demand and software pricing remain unknown.

## What is strong

The main task is concrete: turn confirmed dietary counts and a delivery-inclusive budget into an editable meal plan and packing summary. Quantities carry through allocation, manual correction, approval and confirmation. Preserving edits without rerunning allocation respects the organiser's judgment. Independent server checks and an approved snapshot support confidence that reviewed choices are retained.

The distinction between dietary requirements and optional taste is sound. Unknown meal attributes cannot satisfy a requirement, missing replies do not imply unrestricted diets, and restaurant associations cannot override confirmed restrictions. These are useful correctness controls, not gratuitous clicks. The acceptance record reports automated desktop/mobile workflows and preserved edits through simulated checkout; I did not rerun those checks or observe real users.

## What would kill it: ranked risks

1. **The organiser performs the coordination twice — inference, impact unknown.** Before allocation, someone must already resolve replies, construct mutually exclusive groups, correct overlaps and confirm coverage. Extraction can propose groups, but live-model accuracy is unverified. If most work happens upstream in chat, a polished allocation screen may save too little effort to justify adopting another tool. Static code shows these tasks; it does not establish that users find them difficult.

2. **Taste costs more attention than it returns — inference, benefit unknown.** `TastePreferences.tsx` presents search, exact-branch selection, profile generation and evidence review before allocation. These controls are optional, yet occupy the main Requirements flow. Five house-authored dish mappings and one event-level profile provide a narrow benefit. The recorded Qloo calls changed meal selection; they did not show better acceptance, group agreement or an advantage over choosing a cuisine directly. An organiser's restaurant choices may poorly represent colleagues.

3. **The useful workflow ends before the real task is finished — confirmed prototype boundary; commercial consequence unknown.** A fictional supplier, sample stock and simulated checkout cannot complete an actual team lunch. A real organiser could still have to re-enter the order, reconcile substitutions and communicate packing instructions. The prototype need not build merchant infrastructure now, but its saved plan must prove useful at that handoff before an end-to-end commercial promise is credible.

## Questions for the founder

- Which recurring organiser task should this remove, and what do they currently do to complete it?
- Whose preferences should the restaurant seeds represent, and how will the organiser obtain them?
- What exact output would an organiser and their existing caterer use without retyping or rebuilding the plan?

## Conditions for further investment

Run a small observed pilot with five real organisers using synthetic or consented scenarios. Compare their current method with GroupTable in counterbalanced order; record active time, coaching, corrections and completion. A provisional gate is four uncoached completions, no unresolved count/requirement errors, and lower median active work. Treat this as directional evidence, not demand validation.

Separately compare no taste, direct cuisine selection and restaurant seeds on identical constraints with actual organisers and diners. Keep restaurant lookup prominent only if its extra effort produces a preference benefit they value. Test collapsing that optional section; retain dietary confirmations. Finally, ask one organiser and caterer to review the packing output and identify every remaining manual handoff. No food purchase is required for these tests.

## Hackathon implication

Demonstrate one continuous story: ambiguous counts resolved, feasible allocation, visibly changed Qloo starting choices, manual correction and a retained packing summary. Clearly label the organiser-driven flow, coarse mappings and simulated payment. Keep live extraction and a real Sandbox cycle as separate unverified claims until tested. This supports an honest demonstration; it does not establish competition compliance, commercial demand or a likely win.
